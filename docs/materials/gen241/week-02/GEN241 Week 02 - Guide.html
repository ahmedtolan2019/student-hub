Chapter 2

cin: the program waits for you

After this chapter you can write a prompt and a cin for every input a question gives, and run the program with numbers you type.

2.1A prompt asks, cin reads

Every program on sheet 2 reads its numbers from the keyboard. Each input takes two lines: a cout that asks - it only prints text - and a cin that reads. When the program reaches cin, it stops and waits. Nothing happens until you type a number and press Enter.

console - what you see, typing 250, 8, 20
Enter the distance to drive (km): 250
Enter the fuel efficiency (liters per 100 km): 8
Enter the price (pounds per liter): 20
The fuel cost of the trip is 400 pounds
  1. 1The bold numbers are what you typed. The rest was printed by the program. A program that only asks never gets an answer until you press Enter.
  2. 2cin >> distance; reads a number and puts it in the box called distance. The arrows point the way the number travels: from the keyboard into the variable.
  3. 3The prompt says the unit. A user who does not know the program wants kilometers will type miles.

2.2Several numbers, one cin

One cin can read several numbers, in order: cin >> distance >> efficiency >> price;. You can type them on one line separated by spaces, or press Enter after each - cin does not care, it skips spaces and line breaks and takes the next number each time. This is how the lecture reads two values in its examples.

The order is the only thing that matters. Type 8 250 20 into the line above and the program believes the trip is 8 km and the car burns 250 liters per 100 km.

Key idea

cin fills the variables in the order you wrote them, from whatever you type next, whatever the prompt said.

بالمصري

الـ cout بتسأل بس، والـ cin هي اللي بتقرا. البرنامج بيقف عند الـ cin ويستنى لحد ما تكتب رقم وتدوس Enter.

What could fool you
  • "The program is frozen." A console that just sits there after a prompt is waiting for you, not broken.
  • Commas. Type 4 5, not 4, 5 or (4, 5). The sheet writes points with brackets; cin does not want them.
  • cin << x. The arrows point from the keyboard into the variable: cin >> x. The wrong way round does not compile.

Check yourself

Chapter 3

How much a box holds: int and double

After this chapter you can choose int or double for every quantity in a question, and say what goes wrong if you choose badly.

3.1Whole numbers and numbers with a fraction

A variable is a box with a type, and the type decides what fits. An int holds whole numbers only. A double holds numbers with a fraction. Put 2.5 into an int and the .5 is simply dropped - no error, no warning on most compilers (lecture 3, page 8).

The question to ask for every quantity in a sheet question: can it have a fraction? A distance, a price, a temperature, a radius: yes, so double. A number of days, sides, people: no, so int - and then every division with it needs care (chapter 4).

3.2The trip, typed

Distance, consumption and price can all have a fraction, so the trip uses double for every one of them. The spine at the top of the page shows the price of getting this wrong: make distance an int, divide it by 100, and 250 km becomes 2 hundreds of km instead of 2.5. The fix is not a bigger type; it is the right one.

What could fool you
  • "A bigger type fixes it." long is still a whole number. 2.5 in a long is 2.
  • Copying float from a reference. Some reference solutions use float. It works, with about 7 correct digits instead of 15; prefer double, as the lecture says.
  • Memorising sizes. The sizes depend on the compiler. What does not change: int has no fraction, double does.

Chapter 4

/ and % do different jobs

After this chapter you can predict what any expression on sheet 2 prints before you run it, including every division and remainder.

4.1Two whole numbers, two answers

When both sides of / are whole numbers, C++ answers with a whole number: the quotient, with the fraction thrown away. % gives what was left over. Together they take a number apart, as lecture 3 does with 500 seconds: 500 / 60 is 8 whole minutes, 500 % 60 is the 20 seconds left.

Lecture 3's list, all checked by compiling them: 5 / 2 is 2, -5 / 2 is -2 (toward zero, not down), 5.0 / 2 is 2.5, 26 % 8 is 2, 20 % 13 is 7, 12 % 4 is 0. When the left number is smaller, all of it is the remainder: 3 % 7 is 3.

4.2Shortcuts that change a variable

i += 2 means i = i + 2; the same works for -=, *=, /= and %=. i++ adds one, i-- subtracts one. Starting from k = 10: k -= 4 gives 6, then k *= 3 gives 18, then k /= 4 gives 4 (whole numbers!), then k %= 3 gives 1.

4.3Try it: the calculator and C++ side by side

Type an expression, or press a preset. The left panel does every division the way a calculator does; the right one follows C++'s rule. When they differ, the page says exactly which division lost what. Predict before you press.

4.4Brackets decide the order

Inside an expression, *, / and % happen before + and - (lecture 3, operator precedence). So y2 - y1 / x2 - x1 divides first. A fraction on the sheet needs brackets around its whole top and its whole bottom: Q5's slope is (y2 - y1) / (x2 - x1), and Q6's area is (n * s * s) / (4 * tan(PI / n)).

Key idea

Look at each / on its own: if both sides are whole numbers, the fraction is gone before anything else happens.

بالمصري

الـ / بين رقمين صحاح بتدّي الخارج بس، والـ % بتدّي الباقي. 500 / 60 يبقى 8 دقايق، و 500 % 60 يبقى 20 ثانية.

What could fool you
  • "It rounds." It never rounds: 9 / 5 is 1, not 2, and -5 / 2 is -2.
  • A tidy answer. 320, 132, 139.5: when a messy question gives a round-looking answer, check every division.
  • % with a fraction. 7.5 % 2 does not compile; % is for whole numbers only.

Check yourself

Chapter 5

Where the decimal point has to go

After this chapter you can fix an integer division in the one place that fixes it, with a .0 or a static_cast, and explain why storing the result in a double is too late.

5.1A double box does not save a whole-number division

Lecture 3's Ex_0307 averages 150 points over 16 items. It stores the answer in a double - and still prints 9:

output
Without cast: 9
With static_cast: 9.375
  1. 1C++ works out the right-hand side first. 150 / 16 is two whole numbers, so the answer is 9 - and only then is 9 put into the double box. The box can hold 9.375; it never receives it.
  2. 2static_cast<double>(total_points) turns 150 into 150.0 before the division, so the division is real.
  3. 3Too late is still too late: static_cast<double>(total_points / items) casts the 9 after it was made, and prints 9.

5.2The same trap, in Q1

Q1's formula is fahrenheit = (9 / 5) * celsius + 32, copied from the sheet. With celsius = 100 it prints 132, not 212: 9 / 5 is 1. The fix goes inside the division, where the fraction is lost: 9.0 / 5. Test it with numbers you can check: 100 must give 212 and 37 must give 98.6.

5.3The other way: double into int

Lecture 3 page 8: putting a double into an int drops the fraction, it does not round. static_cast<int>(3.9) is 3, and static_cast<int>(-3.9) is -3. That is what you want for "how many whole years" and never what you want for a price.

Key idea

Put the decimal point, or the cast, on a number inside the division. Afterwards is too late.

Checked by compiling it

9, 9.375 and 9 again for the late cast; 132 and 212 for Q1; 3 and -3 for the casts to int. clang++ -std=c++17 -Wall -Wextra, 4 October 2026. clang warns about int i = 34.7; (lecture 3's own example); Dev-C++ may compile it silently - the value is 34 either way.

بالمصري

الصندوق الـ double مش هينقذ قسمة صحيحة: الـ C++ بتحسب الأول وبعدين تحط النتيجة. حط الـ .0 جوه القسمة نفسها.

What could fool you
  • "The variable is double, so it is fine." The type of the box on the left never reaches back into the division on the right.
  • Fixing one division of two. 15 + 30.0/60 + 30/3600 is still wrong: every / needs its own check.

After this chapter you can turn a paragraph like Q8 into five plan lines, a flowchart and a program whose every block comes from a plan line - and test it with a number you worked out first.

6.1Five lines before the keyboard

Q8: read the distance to drive, the fuel efficiency of the car, and the price per liter, and display the fuel cost of the trip. Small cars are 6 to 12. The sheet calls the efficiency "100 km per liter", but 6 to 12 only makes sense as liters per 100 km - 600 km on one liter is not a car - so that is how the solution reads it.

WANTthe fuel cost of the trip, in pounds
HAVEdistance in km · efficiency in liters per 100 km · price in pounds per liter
CONVERTkm to liters: liters = distance / 100.0 * efficiency
RELATEcost = liters * price
CHECK300 km at 8 L per 100 km is 24 L; at 20 pounds that is 480

Lecture 3 draws programs with four shapes: an oval to start and end, a parallelogram for input and output, a rectangle for a calculation, and arrows for the order. Q8 has no decision, so its flowchart is one straight line - and each shape is one plan line.

6.4Your turn: plan one alone

Not on the sheet: a generator burns 4.5 liters of diesel per hour. It runs for 150 minutes. Diesel costs 15.5 pounds per liter. What does the run cost? Write the five lines in your notebook first, without looking below. Then open the answer.

The five lines, and the trap · open after you have written yours

WANT the cost of the run, in pounds. HAVE 4.5 L per hour, 150 minutes, 15.5 pounds per liter. CONVERT minutes to hours: 150 / 60.0 = 2.5 hours. RELATE liters = 4.5 × 2.5 = 11.25; cost = liters × 15.5. CHECK between the 2-hour and the 3-hour cost.

The program prints 174.375. Written as 150 / 60 it prints 139.5: two whole numbers, so 2 hours, and nothing warns you. Both numbers come from compiling it.
